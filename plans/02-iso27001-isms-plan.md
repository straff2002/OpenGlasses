# ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS readiness and resolution plan

**Date:** 2026-09-04. **Plan status:** Proposed; no actions in this document have been implemented by this assessment. **Assessment type:** Repository-based readiness review, not a certification audit. **Baseline:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022, with Amendment 1:2024. **Accountable sponsor:** Executive responsible for the organization operating OpenGlasses, to be formally appointed.

OpenGlasses has useful security engineering foundations, including Keychain storage, privacy logging regression tests, signed content tooling, dependency pins, and documented remediation work. Repository evidence does not establish an operating information security management system (ISMS). Certification readiness depends on defining the organization and service boundary, obtaining organizational evidence, treating risks, and demonstrating that controls operate over time.

ISO describes ISO/IEC 27001 as an ISMS standard addressing organizational information-security risk. This assessment uses its 2022 edition as the baseline; the 2024 amendment addresses climate considerations. [ISO standard overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001), [ISO amendment record](https://www.iso.org/standard/88435.html).

The clause and Annex A references below are a provisional implementation crosswalk. They identify where original, repository-specific work should be assessed. They do not reproduce the standard's requirements and are not a substitute for checking an authorized copy of ISO/IEC 27001 and applicable implementation guidance with the appointed ISMS lead and auditor. No Annex A exclusion is approved here.

## 1. Scope, assumptions, and evidence rules

### Proposed scope to approve

Start with the organization that develops, releases, licenses, and supports OpenGlasses, including its staff and contractors, developer workstations, source repositories, GitHub Actions, Xcode Cloud and App Store Connect, signing materials, dependency and model acquisition, website and content-pack publication, support/incident handling, and any operator-controlled gateway or license service. Explicitly decide whether each deployed service exists and is included; the repository alone cannot settle this.

Document customer-managed iPhones, glasses, API keys, local networks, gateways, model/provider accounts, and exported files as interfaces in a shared-responsibility matrix. A client-first or bring-your-own-key design changes responsibilities; it does not remove supplier, software, privacy, or support obligations. Customer environments must not be represented as controlled by the vendor unless contractual and technical arrangements support that claim. Record phone, watch, widget, share-extension, glasses SDK, local model, remote inference, MCP, streaming, and medical/field-service surfaces in the architecture inventory.

Treat the medical feature statements in `README.md:369–379` as claims requiring evidence review, not evidence that a medical deployment is compliant. Define supported use cases and prohibited or unsupported deployments with the privacy/AI workstreams before agreeing to contractual security promises.

| Unknown | Organizational or operational evidence was not available in this repository review. The control might already exist elsewhere. Request the evidence before deciding to implement or remediate it. |

## 9. Risk and SoA records to produce

Create a controlled risk register with: risk ID; scenario and affected assets/data subjects; business/security impact; existing controls and evidence confidence; likelihood/impact; inherent and residual rating; legal/contractual requirements; owner; treatment decision; workstream/task; due date; evidence; acceptance authority/date; next review trigger. Distinguish data theft, unauthorized physical action, incorrect safety advice, denial of service and unrecoverable data loss rather than using one generic application-security risk.

For every Annex A reference, the SoA should record: applicability decision; rationale for inclusion or exclusion; associated risk/obligation; implemented control description and boundaries; owner; internal/supplier/customer responsibility; current implementation state; evidence location; effectiveness measure; residual risk; review date. The rows above seed that process; they are **not** an approved SoA. Controls beyond Annex A may be necessary for the app's AI, biometric, medical, agent-action and wearable recording risks.

Required evidence outputs should include scope/context/obligations/RACI, asset and data-flow inventories, policies and acknowledgment records, risk register and approved SoA, secure-development and release records, supplier agreements/assurance, access and device reviews, key lifecycle procedures, incident and continuity exercises, measurement reports, internal audit, management review and corrective-action records. Keep private records outside the repository/Pages publication surface and link them by restricted evidence identifiers.

## 10. Readiness decision and remaining uncertainty

Proceed to a formal readiness review only when the scope and claims are accurate; risks and applicability decisions have accountable approval; critical technical control weaknesses have been treated; control operation has evidence; independent review findings have owners and verified disposition; and management has reviewed the system's performance and resources.

This assessment cannot determine certification status or issue a pass/fail result. The immediate conclusion is that the repository offers substantial implementation material to build on, while the ISMS and its operational evidence remain unestablished in the evidence supplied. Obtain existing off-repository records first, credit what is valid, and implement only the unresolved work through the shared roadmap.
