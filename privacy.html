1. Who we are

OpenGlasses is an iPhone app for smart glasses. It is made by Skunkworks NZ Ltd, a company in New Zealand ("we", "us"). OpenGlasses is proprietary software. This policy explains what the app does with personal information, and it applies to the iPhone app together with its Apple Watch app, widgets and share extension.

We don't run an account system and we don't have a server that collects your conversations or usage data. Section 3 describes the services the app can talk to.

2. What stays on your device

The following is stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone. None of it is sent to us.

  • Conversation history with the assistant. You can lock it behind Face ID or your passcode (Settings, "Encrypt Conversations").
  • Memories and knowledge: things you ask the assistant to remember, the people and topics it links together, and documents you add for it to search.
  • Notes, saved places, location reminders, voice skills, playbooks, teleprompter scripts and study decks, plus any meeting summaries you ask it to save.
  • Faces you teach it. Face recognition keeps a numerical description of each face you enrol, with the name you gave it. It never stores a photo. Matching happens on your phone using Apple's Vision framework.
  • Memory rewind audio. When you turn memory rewind on, the app keeps a rolling buffer of up to the last 10 minutes of audio. The buffer is held in memory and is never saved to storage. If you ask for a recap, the requested minutes are transcribed. The temporary audio file used for that is deleted straight afterwards.
  • Captions. Captions only run once you start them. Recent captions are held in memory while the session runs.
  • Recordings and meeting records that you choose to make, and their transcripts.
  • Knowledge vaults, and Field Assist session logs if you use Field Assist.
  • Usage and cost estimates worked out from each provider's reported token counts, and a log of the app's own network activity that you can view. Both stay on the phone.
  • Settings, and the API keys and sign-in tokens you enter. See Security.

The app's diagnostic logging records only event names, counts, timings and one-way fingerprints. It is built so that it has nowhere to put what you said, what the camera saw, or your keys.

A few stores are excluded from iCloud and device backups so that deleting them really deletes them: conversation history, memories, the knowledge graph, documents and the face database. Your own writing (notes, places, scripts and similar) is included in your normal device backup so it survives moving to a new phone.

You can also pick on-device AI models and on-device speech recognition or voices. When you do, those steps run on your phone and don't use a cloud service.

You deal with these services under their own terms and privacy policies. Most need you to enter your own API key or sign in to your own account with them. We don't receive a copy of what you send.

4. Camera, microphone and people around you

The camera and microphone are used for the features you use. The app listens for its wake word. If you use Apple's speech recognition for this, the app asks for it to run on the device where your language supports it.

Bystander face blur. When you turn on "Blur Bystander Faces" in Settings (it's off by default), the app finds faces on your phone using Apple's Vision framework and blurs them before a camera image leaves the device. That covers images sent to an AI provider, video recordings, live broadcasts, browser streams and expert calls. Two exceptions are deliberate:

  • Face recognition works on the unblurred image, because the blur would hide the very face being matched. That image stays on the phone.
  • Photos you take yourself are saved without the blur.

In video, faces are detected several times a second and the blur follows them in between. Someone stepping into view can therefore be visible briefly before the next detection catches them.

If you record, broadcast or use face recognition around other people, you are responsible for doing so lawfully and with their consent where it is required. You can remove any face you enrolled at any time. See Retention and deletion.

5. Health data

With your permission, the fitness coach reads steps and workouts from Apple Health and can save workouts you log to Apple Health. Step count and walking distance may also come from your phone's motion sensors.

"Share Health Data with AI" is off by default. While it's off, your Apple Health workout history stays on your phone. If you turn it on, the fitness coach can include that history in what it sends to your chosen AI provider so it can discuss your progress. On-device workout tracking, form checking and saving workouts to Apple Health work either way.

If you use the Personal Health Vault (part of the Medical Compliance subscription), the assistant can read the notes you keep there when you ask about them, and send them to your AI provider. You can turn off the assistant's access to the vault in Settings.

We never use health data for advertising, and none of it is sent to us.

6. Medical Compliance mode

Medical Compliance mode (called HIPAA mode in some places in the app) is a setting for people who handle clinical information. When it's on, the app:

  • requires Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to open the app
  • stores clinical recordings and transcripts with iOS's strongest file protection and keeps them out of backups
  • turns off web search, external messaging, cloud memory sync, cloud caption translation and cloud speaker diarization
  • keeps an audit log of access to clinical data on the device
  • deletes clinical data older than the retention period you choose

A further option, "Local LLM Only", sends requests to an on-device model instead of a cloud provider. If no on-device model is available, the request is refused.

This mode is a set of technical safeguards. It is not a certification, and using it does not by itself make an organisation compliant with any law. Organisational duties, such as agreements with any cloud provider you use, remain your responsibility.

7. Permissions

iOS asks for your permission before the app can use each of these. You can change your answer at any time in the iPhone Settings app.

  • Bluetooth: to connect to your glasses.
  • Camera and microphone: to capture photos and video and to hear the wake word and your requests.
  • Speech recognition: to detect the wake word and transcribe what you say.
  • Location: to give answers based on where you are. "Always" access is only used to alert you when you arrive at or leave a place you set a location reminder for.
  • Contacts: to call, message or email people by name.
  • Calendar and Reminders: to tell you about events and create events and reminders.
  • Photos: to save glasses photos and keep them in their own album.
  • Health and Motion: see Health data.
  • HomeKit: to control your smart home.
  • Apple Music: to play and control music.
  • Local network: to find AI servers on your own network and stream to a server on it.

Information from these sources is only sent to an AI provider when it is part of answering your request.

If you believe a child's information has reached us some other way, for example by email, contact us and we will delete it.

9. Retention and deletion

Because your data is on your phone, you control how long it's kept.

  • Conversation history and memories are kept until you delete them, unless you choose a period in Settings, "History" (30 days, 90 days, 6 months or 1 year). Anything older is deleted, not hidden, the next time the app opens or returns to the foreground. You can delete conversations one at a time or all at once from the conversation list.
  • Faces: say "forget [name]" to remove someone. Face recognition can also be switched off.
  • Recordings, notes, places, documents and vaults can be deleted individually in the app. Notes are capped at the 50 most recent.
  • Memory rewind audio is never saved, and it's cleared when you turn the feature off.
  • Export files the app prepares for sharing are temporary, and it deletes them automatically.
  • Deleting the app removes the data in the app's storage. iOS may keep Keychain items after an app is deleted, so remove your API keys and sign out in the app first if you want to be sure those are gone too.

Data you sent to a third-party service is kept under that service's policy. Contact the service to have it deleted.

10. Security

  • API keys and sign-in tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain, on this device only. They are not synced to iCloud or included in backups. Each key is sent only to the service it belongs to.
  • App data is protected by iOS Data Protection. The most sensitive stores (conversations, the face database, clinical data) use the strongest protection class, which makes them unreadable while the phone is locked.
  • "Encrypt Conversations" adds ChaCha20-Poly1305 encryption to saved conversations. The key is held by the Keychain, needs Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to unlock, and the conversations lock again when the app goes to the background.
  • Connections to cloud services use HTTPS. Plain, unencrypted HTTP is only allowed to addresses on your own local network and to your own Tailscale network (ts.net addresses), where Tailscale encrypts the traffic between your devices.
  • You can lock the app's Settings behind Face ID or your passcode.

No system is perfectly secure. If you find a security problem, please report it privately to the contact address below rather than in public.

12. Your rights

Under the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 you have the right to ask for access to personal information we hold about you and to ask us to correct it. We hold very little: usually just what's in any email you send us. Contact us at the address below. For information on your phone, you can view, change and delete it in the app. For information held by a service you chose, contact that service.

If you are not satisfied with how we handle a privacy concern, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner at privacy.org.nz.
