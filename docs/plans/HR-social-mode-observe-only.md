## Why observe-only is the line

Art. 3(39) defines an emotion recognition system by what it *infers*: emotions or intentions of a natural person from their biometric data. The Commission's own contrast case in both the prohibited-practices guidelines and the draft high-risk guidelines is "the mere observation that a person is smiling", which is not emotion recognition. A Social mode that reports what is visible (expression as a visible shape, gaze, posture, gesture, what the person is doing) and never names an emotional state or an intention sits on the observation side of that line. It also happens to be the more honest product: the model cannot know how someone feels, and a neurodivergent wearer is better served by the cues than by a guess.

## P1 — The observation contract (headless)

1. **`SocialObservationContract`** (new pure type beside `AssistiveRouter`): the Social prompt rewritten to describe only what is visible. It asks for: the person's visible expression as shape ("smiling", "mouth turned down", "brow raised", "eyes narrowed"), gaze ("looking at you", "looking at their phone", "looking away"), posture and distance ("leaning in", "arms crossed", "stepping back"), gestures, and what they are doing ("talking", "waiting", "waving you over"). It forbids, in the prompt, naming an emotion, a mood, an intention or a diagnosis, and forbids the words in the filter list below. Urgency is redefined as **situational**, in observable terms: low = nothing needs a response; medium = the person is addressing you or waiting for you; high = the person is signalling urgently (waving you over, pointing, shouting). `userText` becomes "What can you see about the person I'm looking at: their expression, where they're looking, and what they're doing?" The router keywords stay as they are: a wearer who asks "how do they feel" still gets Social mode, and Social mode now answers with what it can see.
   - **Built:** `SocialObservationContract` (`Services/Accessibility/SocialObservationContract.swift`) holds `instructions`, `retryInstruction`, `observationQuestion`, `fallbackLine` and the prompt's sample of forbidden words; `AssistiveRouter.systemPrompt(for: .social)` is the contract plus the unchanged JSON contract and spoken-output fragments. With a transcription, Social mode keeps the wearer's words and appends the observation question after them, so a "how does she feel" request is still answered with what can be seen.
2. **`EmotionLabelFilter`** (new pure type): a deterministic check over the model's `advice` and `followup`. A table of emotional-state and intention words and phrases (happy, sad, angry, anxious, upset, distressed, nervous, bored, frustrated, excited, scared, annoyed, irritated, hostile, friendly-as-a-state, "seems to feel", "is feeling", "wants to", "is about to", "mood", and so on, with simple stemming) decides `.observation` or `.inference(matched:)`. It must not flag the observation vocabulary ("smiling", "frowning", "looking away", "arms crossed", "leaning in"). Tests are a table of sentences both ways, including the near misses ("looks tired" is a physical state and passes; "looks upset" fails).
   - **Built:** `EmotionLabelFilter` (`Services/Accessibility/EmotionLabelFilter.swift`): readable family table `emotionWords`, `inferencePhrases`, whole-word case-insensitive matching with one trailing `s`/`ly`/`ness` stripped. Words that double as observations (`down`, `cross`, `content`, `patient`, bare `want`) are deliberately absent. `EmotionLabelFilterTests` holds the sentence tables both ways.
3. **Fail-closed in the service.** `AssistiveModeService` passes every Social result through the filter. An `.inference` result is not spoken: the service re-asks once with the stricter retry instruction the contract provides; a second `.inference` speaks the fallback "I can describe what I can see, not how they feel." and logs a content-free counter through `PrivacyLog`. Scene mode is untouched. Tests with a fake model: pass-through, retry-then-pass, retry-then-fallback, and the counter.
   - **Built:** `AssistiveModeService.resolveAdvice(mode:systemPrompt:userText:ask:)`, with the model injected as `ask`; counters `socialInferenceRetries` / `socialInferenceWithheld`, logged as `PrivacyLog.vision(.assistiveMode, .inferenceRetried / .inferenceWithheld, count:)`. An unparseable re-ask speaks nothing. `SocialObservationModeTests` drives it with a fake model, including a log tap proving the model's words never reach the log.
4. **Registry and policy.** `.assistiveSocial`'s screening becomes `[.none]` with a `screeningNote` stating that it describes visible expression and posture and infers no emotional state, so it is observation, not emotion recognition under Art. 3(39). The organisation and Field Assist refusals in `AssistiveModePolicy` **stay in this PR**: the legal reason for them is gone, but lifting them should follow device evidence that the filter holds on real frames; record that as the follow-up. The wearer's switch stays.
   - **Built:** `AIFeature.assistiveSocial` screened `[.none]` with a `screeningNote`; `AssistiveModePolicy` unchanged apart from its doc comment recording the follow-up. `.biometric` remains covered by face recognition.

## P2 — Copy and the router gate

5. **Copy.** `SocialModeCopy` standing footer: "Social mode describes what you can see about the person in front of you: their expression, where they're looking and what they're doing. It doesn't guess how they feel." The Accessibility settings and the Assistive toggle use it. `privacy.html`'s Social mode paragraph and the deployer sheet's Art. 50(3) paragraph are rewritten to match: the sheet now says no feature in the app infers emotions, so the Art. 50(3) duty does not arise. The review's §3.2 gains a dated note that Decision 3 was taken and built.
   - **Built:** `SocialModeCopy.standingFooter` rewritten (read by `AccessibilitySettingsView`; the Assistive toggle reads `SocialModeCopy.refusalLine`, unchanged), the Assistive Mode settings footer's "someone in distress" example replaced with "someone waving you over", `privacy.html`, `docs/deployer-information-sheet.md` and a dated note under the review's §3.2.
6. **Router-level feature gate.** `NativeToolRouter.execute` refuses, before dispatch and on every seam, a tool whose name is in `AIFeatureGate.disabledToolNames`, with the feature's standard disabled message, so a switched-off feature is unreachable from Direct mode, Gemini Live, OpenAI Realtime, Siri, skill packs and composed tools alike. Keep the inline checks (defence in depth). Tests: a disabled feature's tool is refused at the router with the same message the inline check gives, an enabled one passes, and `dispatchProfile` reports the refusal class consistently.
   - **Built:** `AIFeatureGate.disabledFeature(forTool:)`; `NativeToolRouter.execute` refuses first (before the egress screen, the policy and every seam) with `.rejected(AIFeatureGate.disabledMessage(feature))` and a `ToolRefusalReason.featureDisabled` security event, and again after an approval if the feature was switched off while the wearer was being asked. `dispatchProfile` returns `.unrouted` for such a tool. `AIFeatureRouterGateTests`.
